\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

This thesis set out to design and verify a compact but complete MIPI I3C Basic Active
Controller suitable for study and extension. This closing chapter recaps what was built
(\cref{sec:contributions}), reflects on the knowledge and skills the work developed
(\cref{sec:lessons}), states the limitations of the implemented system and of its evaluation
(\cref{sec:limitations}), and sets out a prioritised roadmap for future work
(\cref{sec:future-work}).

\section{Summary of contributions}
\label{sec:contributions}

The central contribution is a \emph{functionally complete} Active Controller. Every one of
the \FlowStateCount{} states of the \texttt{flow\_active} command engine is implemented and
exercised (\cref{ch:architecture-rtl,ch:results}), rather than left as future work: the
controller drives SDR private write and read, immediate data transfers, \iic{}-FM legacy
traffic, dynamic address assignment through ENTDAA, and the ENEC/DISEC common command codes.
This is the design result that leads the thesis; the accompanying size comparison in
\cref{ch:results} shows it is achieved in about an order of magnitude less RTL than the
reference, but the completeness of the protocol engine, not the line count, is the headline.

The remaining contributions each map to a chapter:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A study-scale, full-stack controller} spanning the PHY, the protocol engine,
    and the register interface as one coherent, auditable design
    (\cref{ch:architecture-rtl}), with complexity deliberately concentrated in a single
    inspectable FSM rather than spread across a large generated code base.
  \item \textbf{A hand-written control-status register file and 32-entry DAT}
    (\cref{ch:architecture-rtl}) that make the host-visible interface transparent for
    teaching, in contrast to a machine-generated register block.
  \item \textbf{A working dual-agent UVM~1.2 verification environment}
    (\cref{ch:verif-method}) --- a register-bus agent and an I3C-bus agent, a reference-model
    scoreboard, and a set of bound SystemVerilog assertions --- that establishes correctness
    by directed stimulus with coverage-driven extension.
  \item \textbf{Verified evidence of that correctness} (\cref{ch:results}): a fully green
    regression across all runnable scenarios and a functional-coverage result whose residual
    gap is combinatorial breadth rather than any unexercised protocol mode.
\end{itemize}

Taken together, these make the controller, to the author's knowledge, the first
ENTDAA-capable I3C Active Controller developed in the lab.

\section{Knowledge, skills, and lessons learned}
\label{sec:lessons}

Building the controller end-to-end surfaced several lessons that outlast the specific design.

\emph{Treating the code as ground truth prevents drift.} Planning documents and early
specifications inevitably diverge from the implementation as the design matures; adopting a
strict ``code and module specifications win'' policy for every reported fact --- state counts,
port lists, error encodings --- kept the thesis consistent with what actually runs, and made
the register-transfer level, not the prose, the single source of truth.

\emph{A hand-written register file has real educational value.} Writing the CSR and DAT logic
by hand, rather than generating it, forced an explicit understanding of every field, its
reset value, and its side effects; the resulting file is small enough to read in full, which
is precisely what a study design should offer.

\emph{Directed-first bring-up shortens the debugging loop.} Bringing up a state-machine-heavy
controller with deterministic, named scenarios meant that each early failure pointed at one
FSM path rather than at an opaque random draw; constrained randomisation and functional
coverage were then layered on top to measure reach, which proved the right order for a
single-author project.

\emph{Design choices trade simplicity against generality.} The single-counter SCL timing
generator is a clear example: it keeps the timing logic compact and easy to reason about, at
the cost of the finer per-edge programmability a production controller would need. Recognising
such trade-offs explicitly, rather than treating them as omissions, is part of engineering
maturity.

\emph{Diagram-first documentation clarifies the design.} Drawing the FSM and dataflow figures
before writing the surrounding prose repeatedly exposed gaps and inconsistencies in the
mental model of the design, and made the eventual explanation follow the structure of the
hardware.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The limitations fall into two distinct groups: limits of the implemented system, and limits
of how thoroughly it was evaluated. Separating them avoids conflating ``the design does not do
X'' with ``X was not measured''.

\paragraph{Implementation limitations.} The controller implements the SDR and \iic{}-FM
subset only. In-Band Interrupt (IBI), the High-Data-Rate (HDR) modes, Secondary-Controller and
multi-controller operation, and Target mode are all out of scope
(\cref{ch:background-requirements}), as is autonomous bus/error recovery. The design assumes
a single Active Controller on the bus and a modest number of Targets consistent with the
32-entry DAT. These are boundaries of the chosen scope, not defects within it.

\paragraph{Evaluation limitations.} The evaluation is simulation-only. Functional correctness
is established across the verified scenario set, but the coverage result of \cref{ch:results}
is closed only in the single-coverpoint sense: the remaining open bins are the combinatorial
breadth of the automatic cross groups and of the multi-device address-assignment space, which
broader stimulus would still need to reach. The design was not taken through synthesis, so the
target system-clock frequency is a design constraint that this work does not measure, and
there is no FPGA or silicon validation of timing or area. Some subsystems are checked
indirectly through the scoreboard and shared assertions rather than by a dedicated checker.

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future-work}

The roadmap in \cref{tab:future-work} orders the natural extensions of this thesis by
priority. The first tier deepens confidence in the existing design; the second widens the
protocol feature set toward a production controller; the third moves the design onto hardware.

\begin{table}[!htb]\centering
  \caption{Prioritised future-work roadmap.}
  \label{tab:future-work}
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}c >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.26\textwidth}
                    >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.52\textwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Tier} & \textbf{Area} & \textbf{Item and rationale} \\
    \midrule
    1 & Verification breadth & Add constrained-random sweeping and extend the functional-coverage
      model toward full-protocol closure, broadening the CCC, ENTDAA, \iic{}, and
      error-injection sequences toward the full testplan; this directly closes the breadth gap
      identified in \cref{ch:results}. \\
    \midrule
    2 & Protocol features & In-Band Interrupt (IBI) support for Target-initiated events. \\
    2 & Protocol features & High-Data-Rate (HDR) modes for higher throughput. \\
    2 & Protocol features & Secondary-Controller / multi-controller operation with bus handover. \\
    2 & Protocol features & Target mode, so the block can also act as an I3C Target. \\
    2 & Protocol features & Autonomous bus and error recovery. \\
    \midrule
    3 & Silicon validation & FPGA prototyping with a synthesis run to confirm the target
      system-clock frequency, area, and timing left unmeasured by the simulation-only flow. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Pursued in this order, the roadmap turns the study-scale controller of this thesis into a
progressively more complete and hardware-validated I3C Active Controller, while preserving the
readability and directed-verification foundation that motivated the design.
